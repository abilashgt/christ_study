# Daniel's 70 Weeks: Dispensational Timeline

## Intro

The "490 years of Daniel" comes from **Daniel 9:24-27**, the prophecy of the **seventy weeks**.

"Weeks" is commonly understood as **sets of seven**:
- **70 x 7 = 490**
- these are commonly taken as **years**
- so the prophecy is understood as **490 years**

The prophecy is divided into:
- **7 weeks** = 49 years
- **62 weeks** = 434 years
- **1 week** = 7 years

Total:
- **70 weeks = 490 years**

Daniel 9:24 presents these 490 years as relating to:
- finishing transgression
- making an end of sins
- making atonement for iniquity
- bringing in everlasting righteousness
- sealing up vision and prophecy
- anointing the most holy

In **dispensational interpretation**:
- the **69 weeks** lead up to the coming of the **Messiah**
- the **Messiah is cut off**
- the **70th week** is separated by a gap and is still future

## One-Line Chart

```text
445/444 BC (Artaxerxes' decree) -> 7 weeks / 49 years -> 62 weeks / 434 years -> AD 32/33 (end of 69th week; Messiah presented) -> Messiah cut off -> AD 70 (Jerusalem destroyed) -> Gap / Church Age -> Future 70th week / 7-year tribulation -> Midpoint / 3.5 years (covenant broken; sacrifices stop; abomination of desolation) -> End of 70th week (Christ returns; judgment; kingdom established)
```

- **445/444 BC**: decree to rebuild Jerusalem, usually tied to **Artaxerxes' decree in Nehemiah 2**
- **First 69 weeks**: **483 years** total
  This includes:
  - **7 weeks** = 49 years
  - **62 weeks** = 434 years
- **AD 32/33**: end of the **69th week**, often linked to Jesus' presentation as Messiah
- **After the 69th week**:
  - the **Messiah is cut off**: crucifixion
  - Jerusalem is later destroyed: **AD 70**
- **Gap**: the prophetic clock pauses between the **69th and 70th weeks**
  This is usually identified with the **church age**
- **Future 70th week**: **7-year tribulation**
- **Middle of the 70th week**: after **3.5 years**
  - the coming ruler breaks covenant
  - sacrifices stop
  - the **abomination of desolation** occurs
- **End of the 70th week**:
  - Christ returns
  - judgment falls
  - the kingdom is established

| Usual starting point                            | Usually **445/444 BC**, linked to **Nehemiah 2**                               | Often **457 BC**, linked to **Ezra 7**                                                                   | Often treated more schematically, beginning from Jerusalem's fall/exile and moving toward the temple crisis |

| "Messiah cut off"                               | **Jesus' crucifixion** after the 69th week                                     | **Jesus' crucifixion** in the 70th week                                                                  | Often linked to **Onias III** or another anointed figure rather than Jesus                                  |
| Meaning of the 70th week                        | A future **7-year tribulation**                                                | Fulfilled in **Christ's covenant work**, His death, and the transition into the apostolic era            | Fulfilled in the **Antiochus IV** oppression and temple desecration                                         |
| Abomination of desolation                       | A **future antichrist** event in the tribulation                               | Often connected to **AD 70**, or seen as fulfilled first in the Roman destruction and then typologically | Usually identified with **Antiochus IV's desecration of the temple**                                        |

## Summary of Differences

- **Dispensationalism** places the **70th week in the future** and sees a real gap between the 69th and 70th weeks.
- The **Christ-centered / continuous fulfillment** view sees the whole 490 years as running **without a gap**, climaxing in **Jesus' first coming**.
- The **Second-Temple / Maccabean** view does **not** treat the prophecy as pointing directly to a future tribulation, but as reaching fulfillment in the events surrounding **Antiochus IV**.

## Study Note

- Many **historic premillennial**, **amillennial**, and **postmillennial** interpreters reject the dispensational gap and place the **70th week** in the first-century fulfillment connected to Christ.
