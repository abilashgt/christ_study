# Joseph Prince on the Trinity

## Short answer

Joseph Prince publicly identifies with historic Trinitarian Christianity. His ministry’s statement of faith says that there is **one God who eternally exists in three persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit**. It also affirms the Apostles’ Creed and Nicene Creed.

On the evidence of his published doctrinal statement, Joseph Prince is **not** presenting a modalist view—that God is one person who merely appears in three modes—or an Arian view that Jesus is a created being. He affirms the full deity of Jesus and the personal work of the Holy Spirit.

## What he officially believes

### One God in three persons

Prince’s ministry explicitly affirms:

> “We believe there is one God, who eternally exists in three persons—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.”

This is a concise statement of the central Christian doctrine of the Trinity:

- **One God** — Christianity is monotheistic, not tritheistic.
- **Three persons** — the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are personally distinct.
- **Eternally exists** — the three persons are not temporary manifestations or successive stages of God.

### The Father

Prince frequently speaks of God as the believer’s heavenly Father and emphasizes the Father’s love, provision, welcome, and desire to give grace to His children. He presents the Father as the one who sent the Son and whose heart is revealed through Jesus.

Relevant Scriptures: John 3:16; John 5:19–23; John 14:8–11; Ephesians 1:3–6.

### The Son, Jesus Christ

The statement of faith affirms Jesus’ deity, virgin birth, sinless life, miracles, atoning death, bodily resurrection, ascension to the Father’s right hand, present ministry as High Priest and Mediator, and future return.

Prince’s wider theology is strongly Christ-centered: the believer receives righteousness, forgiveness, healing, and access to God through Jesus’ finished work on the cross.

Relevant Scriptures: John 1:1–18; John 8:58; John 10:30; Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–20; Hebrews 1:1–4.

### The Holy Spirit

Prince’s statement calls the Holy Spirit the believer’s Comforter and says that the Spirit guides believers. It also affirms baptism in the Holy Spirit, praying in the Spirit, the nine gifts of the Spirit, and the fruit of the Spirit.

This indicates a charismatic understanding of the Holy Spirit as a divine and personal agent, not merely an impersonal force. Prince’s ministry expects the ongoing operation of spiritual gifts in the church.

Relevant Scriptures: John 14:16–17, 26; John 16:7–15; Acts 2:1–4; 1 Corinthians 12:4–11; Ephesians 4:30.

## Biblical pattern used to explain the Trinity

The word *Trinity* does not appear in the Bible, but the doctrine summarizes several biblical truths held together:

| Biblical truth | Representative Scriptures |
|---|---|
| There is one God | Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 45:5–6; 1 Corinthians 8:4–6 |
| The Father is God | John 6:27; 1 Corinthians 8:6; Ephesians 4:6 |
| The Son is God | John 1:1; John 20:28; Titus 2:13; Hebrews 1:8 |
| The Holy Spirit is God | Acts 5:3–4; 1 Corinthians 3:16; 2 Corinthians 3:17–18 |
| The persons are distinct | Matthew 3:16–17; John 14:16–17; John 17:1–5 |
| Father, Son, and Spirit are named together | Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14; 1 Peter 1:2 |

## Relationship to Prince’s wider theology

Prince’s distinctive emphases are not primarily anti-Trinitarian. His controversial teachings concern the new covenant, law and grace, healing, prosperity/provision, positive confession, sin-consciousness, and Communion. On the Trinity itself, his published position is broadly consistent with the Nicene and orthodox Protestant understanding of God.

His emphasis on the Father’s love and the Son’s finished work can be pastorally attractive. However, the truth of a preacher’s Trinitarian confession does not by itself validate all of his other doctrines. Each teaching still needs to be tested against the whole counsel of Scripture (Acts 17:11; 2 Timothy 3:16–17).

## Conclusion

**Joseph Prince believes in the historic Trinity:** one eternal God in three distinct persons—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. He affirms the deity of Jesus, the personal and active ministry of the Holy Spirit, and the Apostles’ and Nicene Creeds. Based on his public statement of faith, his doctrine of the Trinity should be classified as orthodox, even though other parts of his theology remain controversial.
