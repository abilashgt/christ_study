# Jeremiah's 70 Years

## Intro

Jeremiah's prophecy says Judah would experience a period of exile under Babylon before restoration.

The **70 years** are usually understood as the period of Babylonian dominance, Judah's captivity, and the land's desolation leading up to return and restoration.

The exile also has a covenant reason: Israel had neglected the land's **sabbatical rest**, so the land would now enjoy its Sabbaths during the desolation. That is why **2 Chronicles 36:21** connects the exile to the fulfillment of the word of the LORD.

In short, the 70-year exile is both:
- a judgment for covenant disobedience
- a time for the land to receive its overdue Sabbath rest

## Main Texts

- **Jeremiah 25:11-12**: Judah and the nations will serve Babylon for **70 years**
- **Jeremiah 29:10**: after **70 years** Babylon will be visited and God's people will be brought back
- **2 Chronicles 36:20-23**: the exile and the decree of Cyrus are tied together
- **Ezra 1:1-4**: Cyrus issues the decree for return and rebuilding
- **Daniel 9:1-3**: Daniel reads Jeremiah's prophecy and prays for restoration
- **Leviticus 26:34-35**: the land will enjoy its Sabbaths while it lies desolate

## Simple Timeline

```text
605/604 BC (first Babylonian deportation) -> 586 BC (Jerusalem and the temple destroyed) -> 539 BC (Babylon falls to Persia) -> 538 BC (Cyrus' decree) -> return from exile -> rebuilding and restoration
```

## Timeline

- **605/604 BC**: first Babylonian deportation begins
- **586 BC**: Jerusalem and the temple are destroyed
- **539 BC**: Babylon falls to Persia
- **538 BC**: Cyrus issues the decree allowing the Jews to return (**Ezra 1:1-4**)
- **516 BC**: the second temple is completed

## Why The 70 Years Matter

- They show that God judges sin but does not abandon His covenant people.
- They frame the exile as a limited and purposeful discipline.
- They provide the historical background for **Daniel 9**, where Daniel understands the end of Jeremiah's seventy years to be near and prays for mercy.

## Connection To Sabbatical Years

- The **70 years of exile** are often connected to **70 missed sabbatical years**.
- If Israel neglected the land's Sabbath rest for **490 years**, that would amount to **70 sabbatical years** missed.
- The exile is therefore understood as a time when the land finally receives its rest.
- Key passages:
  - **Leviticus 26:34-35**: the land will enjoy its Sabbaths while it lies desolate
  - **2 Chronicles 36:21**: the exile fulfills the word of the LORD so the land could enjoy its Sabbaths

In that sense, the 70-year exile is not random. It is tied to covenant disobedience and the land's overdue Sabbath rest.

## Connection To Daniel 9

Daniel's prayer in **Daniel 9:1-3** is based on Jeremiah's seventy-year promise.

That means Daniel 9 does not appear in isolation. It stands on the expectation that:

- the exile would end
- God would restore His people
- Jerusalem and the temple would be rebuilt
- covenant mercy would follow judgment

## Connection To Daniel 9: 70 And 490

- **Jeremiah's 70 years** are the immediate prophecy Daniel is reading in **Daniel 9:1-3**.
- **Daniel's 70 weeks** in **Daniel 9:24-27** are the larger prophetic answer that follows Daniel's prayer.
- The **70 years** are often tied to **70 missed sabbatical years**.
- **70 missed sabbatical years x 7 years each = 490 years** of neglected sabbatical rhythm.
- So the **70 years of exile** and the **490 years** are connected through the idea of covenant disobedience to the land's Sabbath rest.

In short:

- **70 years** = the exile and restoration background
- **490 years** = the larger prophetic framework that Daniel receives in response

## Study Note

- **Jeremiah 70 years** is the background.
- **Daniel 70 weeks** is the larger prophetic answer that follows from that background.
- The two passages belong together in the prophetic storyline of judgment, exile, repentance, and restoration.
