# Comparison of Premillennialism, Postmillennialism, and Amillennialism

## Main Question

These three views mainly differ on how they understand the "1000 years" in [[Revelation 20]] and how that period relates to the return of Christ.

## Quick Summary

### Premillennialism
- Christ returns **before** the millennium.
- The millennium is usually understood as a future reign of Christ on earth.
- Satan is bound in a fuller sense during that future kingdom.

### Postmillennialism
- Christ returns **after** the millennium.
- The millennium is usually understood as a long period of gospel success and widespread righteousness in history.
- The kingdom grows in history before Christ's return.

### Amillennialism
- There is no separate future earthly 1000-year kingdom after Christ returns.
- The millennium is usually understood as the present church age, in which Christ reigns from heaven.
- Christ returns once at the end of this age, followed by resurrection and judgment.

## Comparison Table

| Topic | Premillennialism | Postmillennialism | Amillennialism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning of "millennium" | Usually a future, literal 1000-year reign of Christ on earth | Usually a long golden age of gospel blessing, not necessarily a literal 1000 years | Usually symbolic of the present church age |
| When does Christ return? | **Before** the millennium | **After** the millennium | **After** the present millennial age |
| View of Revelation 20 | Mostly future | Often future in effect, but interpreted more symbolically | Usually present reality between Christ's first and second comings |
| Where is Christ reigning? | On earth during the millennium | From heaven over a gospel-transformed world | From heaven now over His church and kingdom |
| State of the world before Christ returns | Often increasing evil and crisis before Christ's return | Increasing gospel influence and righteousness before Christ's return | Mixture of gospel advance, suffering, and opposition throughout this age |
| Satan's binding | Future and strong restraint during the millennium | Often connected to the success of the gospel in history | Present restraint so the nations are not deceived in the same way as before |
| Resurrection | Usually a resurrection before the millennium and another after | Generally one general resurrection at Christ's return | Generally one general resurrection at Christ's return |
| Final judgment | After the millennium | At Christ's return after the millennium | At Christ's return at the end of this age |
| Kingdom emphasis | Future earthly kingdom | Kingdom progressively filling the earth in history | Kingdom already present now, awaiting final consummation |
| General tone about history | Often pessimistic about the course of the age | More optimistic about the course of the age | Realistic tension: victory in Christ, but conflict continues until the end |
| Main proponents in church history | Papias, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Lactantius | Daniel Whitby, Jonathan Edwards, Charles Hodge, A. A. Hodge, B. B. Warfield | Tyconius, Augustine, Anselm, Thomas Aquinas, Martin Luther, John Calvin |

## Historic Premillennialism vs Futuristic Premillennialism

This table compares two major premillennial approaches. In many church discussions, "futuristic premillennialism" usually refers to **dispensational premillennialism**.

| Topic | Historic Premillennialism | Futuristic Premillennialism |
|---|---|---|
| Basic view | Christ returns before a future millennium | Christ returns before a future millennium |
| Reading of prophecy | Often mixes literal and symbolic interpretation | Usually emphasizes a more consistently literal reading, especially for Israel and end-time prophecy |
| Israel and the church | Usually sees one people of God across redemptive history | Usually makes a stronger distinction between Israel and the church |
| Tribulation | Church usually passes through tribulation | Often teaches a future seven-year tribulation distinct from the church age |
| Rapture | Usually tied closely to Christ's visible second coming | Often distinguished from the visible second coming, especially in pre-tribulational systems |
| Second coming | Generally one main return of Christ before the millennium | Often presented in phases: rapture, then later visible return in glory |
| Millennium | Future earthly reign of Christ | Future earthly reign of Christ |
| Resurrection | Often sees believers raised at Christ's return, with final judgment afterward | Often includes more detailed resurrection stages in the end-times sequence |
| Revelation 20 | Future and usually straightforwardly millennial | Future and part of a larger end-times framework including tribulation and kingdom |
| Daniel and Revelation | Important, but not always mapped into a highly detailed timetable | Often arranged into a more detailed prophetic chronology |
| Kingdom promises | Fulfilled in Christ and His reign, often including the church in those promises | Often emphasizes national Israel's future restoration in a distinct way |
| Common tone | Older, church-historical premillennial view | More systematized prophetic framework, especially in dispensational teaching |
| Main proponents in church history | Papias, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Lactantius, George Eldon Ladd | John Nelson Darby, C. I. Scofield, Lewis Sperry Chafer, John Walvoord, Charles Ryrie |
| Current/recent proponents | John Piper, Craig L. Blomberg, Douglas Moo | John MacArthur, Craig Blaising, Mark Snoeberger |

## Simple Timeline

### Premillennialism
- Church age
- Christ returns
- Millennium
- Final judgment
- Eternal state

### Postmillennialism
- Church age with increasing gospel success
- Millennium
- Christ returns
- Final judgment
- Eternal state

### Amillennialism
- Church age = millennium
- Christ returns
- Resurrection and final judgment
- Eternal state

## Common Ground

All three views affirm:
- Jesus Christ will return bodily and visibly.
- There will be a final judgment.
- God's kingdom will be fully and finally established.
- Believers will dwell with Christ forever.

## Study Notes

- The biggest interpretive issue is whether [[Revelation 20]] should be read as describing a **future earthly kingdom** or a **symbolic description of the present age**.
- Premillennialism emphasizes the future order of events in [[Revelation 19]] and [[Revelation 20]].
- Postmillennialism emphasizes the power of the gospel to transform the nations in history.
- Amillennialism emphasizes the "already and not yet" nature of Christ's kingdom.
- Within premillennialism, there are important differences between **historic premillennialism** and **dispensational premillennialism**.
