# American Church History

American church history is the story of how Christianity developed in the colonies and United States through settlement, revival, denominational growth, missions, reform, and modern fragmentation.

| Period | Approx. Date | Church Figures | Outside Church | Key Events | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Colonial Era | 1607-1775 | John Cotton, Jonathan Edwards, George Whitefield | John Smith, William Bradford, Benjamin Franklin | Jamestown, Puritan settlements, Great Awakening, colonial expansion | Colonial churches are shaped by settlement, establishment, revival, and denominational beginnings |
| Revolutionary Era | 1775-1789 | Samuel Davies, Francis Asbury, Isaac Backus | George Washington, Thomas Jefferson, John Adams, Thomas Paine | Independence, Constitution, Bill of Rights | Churches adjust to religious liberty and disestablishment |
| Early Republic | 1789-1860 | Lyman Beecher, Charles Finney, Peter Cartwright | Andrew Jackson, James Monroe, Abraham Lincoln | Westward expansion, Second Great Awakening, reform movements | Revivalism, camp meetings, and reform movements reshape American Protestantism |
| Civil War Era | 1860-1877 | Henry Ward Beecher, Dwight L. Moody | Abraham Lincoln, Ulysses S. Grant, Jefferson Davis | Civil War, Emancipation Proclamation, Reconstruction | Churches are divided by slavery and then pushed toward reconciliation and social action |
| Gilded Age | 1877-1900 | Moody, Frances Willard, A. B. Simpson | Andrew Carnegie, Mark Twain, Theodore Roosevelt | Industrialization, urbanization, missions, social reform | Churches respond to urban poverty, labor, holiness teaching, and missionary expansion |
| Progressive and World War Era | 1900-1945 | Billy Sunday, Aimee Semple McPherson, J. Gresham Machen | Woodrow Wilson, Franklin D. Roosevelt, Herbert Hoover | Progressive reforms, World Wars, Great Depression | Fundamentalist-modernist conflict intensifies across denominations |
| Postwar America | 1945-2000 | Billy Graham, Martin Luther King Jr., Carl F. H. Henry, Francis Schaeffer, Charles Stanley, John MacArthur | Harry Truman, Dwight Eisenhower, John F. Kennedy, Ronald Reagan | Civil rights, suburban growth, Cold War, culture wars | Evangelicalism gains major influence in churches and public life |
| Contemporary Era | 2000-Present | Rick Warren, Timothy Keller, Beth Moore | George W. Bush, Barack Obama, Donald Trump, Joe Biden | 9/11, polarization, digital media, immigration, renewed secularization debates | American church life becomes more fragmented, networked, and digitally mediated |
