# Catholicism vs. Eastern Orthodoxy vs. Reformed Baptist

## Primary Issues

| Question                      | Catholicism                                                                                                                                                                           | Eastern Orthodoxy                                                                                                                                                        | Reformed Baptist                                                                                                                                                   | Comments                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Source of salvation**       | God’s grace through Jesus Christ<br>• Eph 2:8–9; Acts 4:12                                                                                                                              | God’s grace through Jesus Christ<br>• Eph 2:8–9; Acts 4:12                                                                                                                 | God’s grace through Jesus Christ<br>• Eph 2:8–9; Acts 4:12                                                                                                           | All three affirm that salvation is God’s grace through Jesus Christ.                                                    |
| **How salvation is received** | Faith working through love within the Church and its sacraments<br>• James 2:24; John 3:5; Gal 5:6                                                                                      | Faith, repentance, and participation in the life of the Church<br>• James 2:24; John 3:5; Gal 5:6                                                                          | Faith alone in Christ alone<br>• Rom 3:28; Eph 2:8–9; Phil 3:9                                                                                                       | The dispute concerns faith’s relationship to sacramental life and works.                                                |
| **Justification**             | God makes the believer righteous through grace<br>• James 2:24                                                                                                                          | Emphasizes healing, transformation, and union with God<br>• James 2:24                                                                                                     | God declares sinners righteous through Christ’s imputed righteousness<br>• Rom 4:5; Gal 2:16                                                                         | The disagreement is whether justification is primarily declarative, transformative, or both.                            |
| **Role of good works**        | Necessary fruit of grace and love<br>• James 2:14–26; Gal 5:6                                                                                                                           | Part of faithful cooperation with divine grace<br>• James 2:14–26; Gal 5:6                                                                                                 | Evidence of genuine faith, not the basis of justification<br>• Rom 3:28; Eph 2:8–9                                                                                   | The key distinction is whether works are the basis of justification or the necessary fruit of genuine faith.            |
| **Final authority** | Scripture and Sacred Tradition, interpreted by the Pope and bishops<br>• Matt 16:18–19; 1 Tim 3:15 | Scripture and Holy Tradition, received through the bishops and councils<br>• Acts 15; 1 Tim 3:15 | Scripture as the final authority<br>• 2 Tim 3:16–17; Acts 17:11 | The question is what constitutes final authority and how the Church interprets Scripture. |
| **Purgatory**                 | Teaches a post-death purification for those who die in God’s grace but are not fully purified<br>• 1 Cor 3:13–15; 2 Macc 12:44–46                                                       | Rejects the Catholic doctrine as formally defined; teaches an intermediate state and prayer for the departed<br>• 2 Macc 12:44–46                                          | Rejects purgatory; teaches judgment after death and the final resurrection<br>• Heb 9:27; Luke 23:43                                                                 | The texts are disputed, especially regarding post-death purification and the state of the departed.                     |
| **Baptism**                   | Sacrament that ordinarily begins new life in Christ and incorporates a person into the Church; infants may be baptized<br>• John 3:5; Acts 2:38; 1 Pet 3:21; Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16 | Mystery through which a person enters the Church and receives new life in Christ; infants may be baptized<br>• John 3:5; Acts 2:38; 1 Pet 3:21; Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16 | Ordinance symbolizing union with Christ; given only to professing believers and not the basis of justification<br>• Acts 2:41; Rom 6:3–4                             | The debate is infant inclusion versus baptism following a personal profession of faith.                                 |
| **Confession and repentance** | Sacramental confession to a priest is ordinarily required after serious post-baptismal sin<br>• John 20:23; James 5:16                                                                  | Confession and repentance before God, normally with a priest or spiritual father, restore communion with the Church<br>• John 20:23; James 5:16                            | Repentance and confession are directed to God; pastoral accountability may be practiced, but there is no sacramental priesthood<br>• 1 John 1:9; Heb 4:16; 1 Tim 2:5 | The dispute concerns whether John 20 authorizes sacramental priestly absolution or emphasizes direct confession to God. |
| **Human response**            | Faith cooperates with grace<br>• Phil 2:12–13; 2 Pet 1:5                                                                                                                                | Humans cooperate with divine grace, often called **synergy**<br>• Phil 2:12–13; 2 Pet 1:5                                                                                  | Faith is God’s gift, received through repentance and trust in Christ<br>• John 6:44; Eph 2:8–9; Rom 9:16                                                             | The debate concerns divine initiative and human cooperation.                                                            |
| **Perseverance**              | A person must continue in faith and charity<br>• Heb 6:4–6; John 15:6                                                                                                                   | Emphasizes continuing in faith and communion with God<br>• Heb 6:4–6; John 15:6                                                                                            | Usually teaches preservation and perseverance of the saints<br>• John 10:28–29; Rom 8:30; Phil 1:6                                                                   | The debate concerns whether warning passages describe loss of salvation or God’s means of preserving believers.         |
| **Ultimate goal**             | Eternal communion with God<br>• 1 Cor 13:12; 2 Pet 1:4                                                                                                                                  | Communion with God and transformation into his likeness, called **theosis**<br>• 1 Cor 13:12; 2 Pet 1:4                                                                    | Glorification with Christ and eternal life with God<br>• Rom 8:30; Phil 3:20–21                                                                                      | All await resurrection and communion with God, while emphasizing different biblical descriptions of that hope.          |

All three affirm that salvation comes from God’s grace through Jesus Christ. Their differences concern how justification, baptism, repentance, confession, works, and perseverance relate to salvation. The Scripture references appear directly after each tradition’s position; many of these passages are interpreted differently across traditions.

## Secondary or Peripheral Issues

| Issue | Catholicism | Eastern Orthodoxy | Reformed Baptist | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Communion** | Christ truly present; transubstantiation<br>• John 6:53–56; 1 Cor 10:16 | Christ truly present as a mystery<br>• John 6:53–56; 1 Cor 10:16 | Spiritual communion with Christ; usually not physical presence<br>• Luke 22:19; 1 Cor 11:24 | The dispute concerns how Christ’s ‘body and blood’ language should be understood. |
| **Mary and the saints** | Honored; saints may be asked to intercede<br>• Luke 1:48; Rev 5:8 | Strongly honored; saints may be asked to intercede<br>• Luke 1:48; Rev 5:8 | Honored as examples; prayer is directed to God alone<br>• 1 Tim 2:5 | The dispute concerns honoring saints versus invoking them and the role of Christ as mediator. |
| **Worship style** | Mass and established liturgy<br>• Acts 2:42; Rev 4–5 | Ancient Divine Liturgy<br>• Acts 2:42; Rev 4–5 | Usually preaching-centered worship<br>• Acts 2:42; 1 Cor 14:26 | Both approaches can claim biblical warrant; the difference is continuity, structure, and form. |
| **Church governance** | Universal, hierarchical church under the Pope<br>• Matt 16:18–19; Titus 1:5 | Communion of self-governing churches<br>• Acts 15; 1 Pet 5:1–3 | Congregational churches led by pastors and elders<br>• 1 Pet 5:1–3; Acts 14:23 | The dispute concerns universal, conciliar, and congregational governance. |
| **Number of sacraments or ordinances** | Seven sacraments<br>• Various New Testament sacramental texts | Seven mysteries<br>• Various New Testament sacramental texts | Two ordinances<br>• Matt 28:19; 1 Cor 11:23–26 | The traditions differ over whether the New Testament presents a broader sacramental pattern or only two explicit ordinances. |
| **Clergy** | Latin-rite priests normally celibate<br>• 1 Cor 7; Matt 19:12 | Married men may become priests; bishops are celibate<br>• 1 Tim 3:2; Titus 1:6 | Pastors and elders may marry<br>• 1 Tim 3:2; Titus 1:6 | Scripture permits married ministers but does not require marriage or celibacy. |
| **Church membership** | Includes baptized infants<br>• Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16 | Includes baptized infants<br>• Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16 | Normally limited to those who credibly profess faith<br>• Acts 2:41–42 | No passage explicitly settles infant church membership, so the debate turns on how household baptisms and profession of faith are read. |
