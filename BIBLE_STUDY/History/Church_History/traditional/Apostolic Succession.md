# Apostolic Succession

## Explicit Scriptures - Scope

The phrase **apostolic succession** does not occur in Scripture. The passages below are limited to texts that explicitly address apostolic office, appointment of church leaders, transmission of ministry, or limits on ministerial authority.

| Scripture                                      | Explicit statement                                                                   | For Apostic Succession                                | Against Apostic Succession                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Acts 1:15–26                                   | Matthias replaces Judas: “Let another take his office.”                              | Explicit replacement within the apostolic office.     | This concerns the unique Twelve and does not explicitly establish permanent episcopal succession.                |
| Acts 14:23                                     | Elders are appointed in every church.                                                | Continuing appointment of church leaders.             | Elders are appointed, but the text does not call them successors to the apostles.                                |
| Acts 20:17, 28                                 | Elders are made overseers by the Holy Spirit.                                        | Establishes the office of overseer.                   | The passage does not describe an unbroken succession of bishops.                                                 |
| 1 Timothy 3:1–13                               | Qualifications are given for overseers and deacons.                                  | Recognized church offices.                            | It defines qualifications, not apostolic succession.                                                             |
| 1 Timothy 4:14                                 | Timothy receives a gift through the laying on of hands by elders.                    | Formal transmission of ministry.                      | It does not explicitly require a continuing chain of bishops.                                                    |
| 1 Timothy 5:22                                 | “Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands.”                                         | Controlled ministerial appointment.                   | Laying on of hands alone does not prove apostolic succession.                                                    |
| 2 Timothy 1:6                                  | Timothy receives a gift through Paul’s laying on of hands.                           | Apostolic commissioning of Timothy.                   | This is a specific relationship between Paul and Timothy, not an explicit universal rule.                        |
| 2 Timothy 2:2                                  | Paul’s teaching is entrusted to Timothy, faithful men, and others.                   | Multi-generational transmission of ministry teaching. | It explicitly concerns transmission of teaching, not necessarily succession of office.                           |
| Titus 1:5–9                                    | Titus appoints elders in every town.                                                 | An apostolic delegate establishes church leadership.  | Titus appoints elders, but they are not explicitly called apostolic successors.                                  |
| Philippians 1:1                                | Churches have overseers and deacons.                                                 | Established local offices.                            | No claim is made that these offices continue through an apostolic lineage.                                       |
| 1 Peter 5:1–4                                  | Elders shepherd the flock under Christ.                                              | Continuing pastoral leadership.                       | Christ remains the Chief Shepherd; human succession is not presented as essential.                               |
| 2 Timothy 3:16–17; Acts 17:11; Galatians 1:8–9 | Scripture equips believers; teaching must be tested; false gospels must be rejected. | Limits all church authority.                          | These passages oppose treating succession as self-authenticating, though they do not explicitly deny succession. |

## Strict conclusion

Scripture explicitly teaches:

1. Replacement within an apostolic office — **Acts 1:20–26**.
2. Transmission of apostolic teaching — **2 Timothy 2:2**.
3. Appointment of elders and overseers — **Acts 14:23; Titus 1:5**.
4. Laying on of hands in ministry — **1 Timothy 4:14; 2 Timothy 1:6**.
5. Limits on all ministerial authority — **2 Timothy 3:16–17; Acts 17:11; Galatians 1:8–9**.

Scripture does not explicitly state:

- the phrase “apostolic succession”;
- that bishops are successors to the apostles;
- that there must be an uninterrupted historical line of bishops; or
- that every bishop in such a line is doctrinally infallible.

Therefore, the biblical evidence explicitly establishes appointed church leadership and transmission of apostolic teaching. The stronger claim that this necessarily requires an unbroken succession of bishops is a theological conclusion, not an explicit biblical statement.

## Early Church History

The phrase **apostolic succession** does not appear as a technical expression in the earliest surviving writings. Early Church Fathers used related expressions such as “succeed to their ministry,” “succession from the apostles,” and “succession of bishops.”

| Approximate date | Church Father | Text | Explicit historical detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. AD 96 | Clement of Rome | *1 Clement* 42, 44 | The apostles appointed bishops and deacons and made provision that approved men should succeed them in their ministry. |
| c. AD 107–110 | Ignatius of Antioch | *Letter to the Magnesians* 6:1 | The bishop presides “in the place of God,” while the presbyters are compared with the council of the apostles. |
| c. AD 180 | Irenaeus of Lyons | *Against Heresies* 3.3.1–3 | Apostolic teaching is traced through the succession of bishops, including the succession of the Roman church. |
| c. AD 180 | Irenaeus of Lyons | *Against Heresies* 4.26.2; 4.33.8 | The presbyters who possess succession from the apostles are associated with the preservation of truth and the Church. |
| c. AD 200–210 | Tertullian of Carthage | *Prescription Against Heretics* 32 | Heretical churches are challenged to produce their bishops in succession from the apostles or apostolic men. |

### Historical conclusion

- **Earliest explicit patristic statement:** Clement of Rome, approximately **AD 96**.
- **First fully developed episcopal-succession argument:** Irenaeus, approximately **AD 180**.
- **Exact technical phrase “apostolic succession”:** likely a later theological expression; the earliest Fathers used equivalent language rather than the exact phrase.
