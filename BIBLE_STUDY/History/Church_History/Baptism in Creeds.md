# Baptism in Major Creeds and Confessions

This is a curated historical table, not a literal inventory of every local creed or confession ever written. It focuses on the major ecumenical creeds and the most influential confessional standards that either mention baptism explicitly or shape baptismal doctrine.

| Date               | Document                                       | Tradition                     | Baptism Mention?    | What It Says About Baptism                                                                                                               |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| c. 2nd-4th century | Apostles' Creed                                | Early ecumenical              | No explicit wording | Often used in baptismal catechesis, but the standard text does not directly discuss baptism.                                             |
| 325                | Nicene Creed                                   | Ecumenical                    | No explicit wording | The original Nicene Creed centers on the Trinity and Christology, not baptism.                                                           |
| 381                | Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed                | Ecumenical                    | Yes                 | Confesses "one baptism for the forgiveness of sins." This is the clearest baptism line in the ancient creeds.                            |
| 5th-6th century    | Athanasian Creed                               | Ecumenical                    | No explicit wording | Strong on Trinitarian and Christological orthodoxy; baptism is not a stated theme.                                                       |
| 451                | Chalcedonian Definition                        | Ecumenical                    | No explicit wording | Defines Christ's two natures; does not address baptism.                                                                                  |
| 1536               | Ten Articles                                   | English Reformation precursor | Yes                 | Treats baptism as necessary, connected to remission of sins, regeneration, and infant baptism.                                           |
| 1530               | Augsburg Confession                            | Lutheran                      | Yes                 | Article 9 teaches that baptism is necessary and gives grace; it also defends baptism of children.                                        |
| 1536               | First Helvetic Confession                      | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Describes baptism as a sign that corresponds to regeneration and adoption.                                                               |
| 1559               | French Confession of Faith (Gallic Confession) | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Treats baptism as a covenant sign and seal and defends infant baptism.                                                                   |
| 1561               | Belgic Confession                              | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Article 34 presents baptism as the sign and seal of cleansing, new life, and covenant membership; it supports infant baptism.            |
| 1560/1563          | Scots Confession                               | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Baptism is treated as a covenant sign that marks belonging to Christ and is applied to believers and their children.                     |
| 1563               | Heidelberg Catechism                           | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Baptism is a sign and seal of forgiveness, cleansing, and incorporation into Christ; it strongly affirms infant baptism.                 |
| 1566               | Second Helvetic Confession                     | Reformed                      | Yes                 | Baptism is a sign and seal of regeneration and adoption, and the confession argues for the baptism of infants.                           |
| 1571               | Thirty-Nine Articles                           | Anglican                      | Yes                 | Article 27 says baptism is a sign of regeneration or new birth and that infant baptism should be retained.                               |
| 1615               | Irish Articles of Religion                     | Anglican / Reformed           | Yes                 | Baptism seals regeneration, justification, forgiveness, adoption, and sanctification; infant baptism is affirmed.                        |
| 1646               | Westminster Confession of Faith                | Reformed / Presbyterian       | Yes                 | Baptism is a sign and seal of the covenant of grace, union with Christ, regeneration, and remission of sins; infant baptism is affirmed. |
| 1689               | Baptist Confession of Faith                    | Particular Baptist            | Yes                 | Baptism is for professing believers, normally by immersion, and is not given to infants.                                                 |
| 1784               | Articles of Religion                           | Methodist                     | Yes                 | Baptism is a sign of profession and difference, and also a sign of regeneration or new birth; infant baptism is retained.                |

## Quick Takeaways

- The ancient ecumenical creeds only explicitly mention baptism in the 381 Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed.
- The later Reformation confessions give much fuller baptismal theology.
- The biggest dividing lines are infant baptism, baptismal regeneration language, and whether baptism is a sign only or a sign plus means of grace.
