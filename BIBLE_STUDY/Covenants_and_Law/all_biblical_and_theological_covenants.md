# Covenants in the Bible

## What is a covenant?

A covenant is a solemn, binding relationship established by promises, obligations, blessings, warnings, and sometimes a covenant sign. Biblical covenants may be made by God, between people, or between nations.

Some classifications below are explicit in Scripture; others are theological categories used to summarize the Bible's storyline. The exact number of biblical covenants varies by tradition.

## Theological covenants in covenant theology

These terms summarize the Bible's covenant structure. The phrases **Covenant of Works** and **Covenant of Grace** are theological categories; Scripture does not use them as formal titles in the same way it uses “New Covenant” or “Davidic covenant.”

### Covenant of Works

The Covenant of Works describes God's covenant arrangement with Adam before the Fall. Adam was created upright, given a command, and promised life in the way of perfect obedience; disobedience brought death and judgment.

**Key passages:** Genesis 2:15–17; Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45–49; Hosea 6:7.

The classification is debated, especially because Genesis 2 does not explicitly use the word *covenant*. It is also called the **Covenant of Creation** or **Covenant of Life** by some theologians.

### Covenant of Grace

The Covenant of Grace describes God's gracious promise to save sinners through faith in the promised Redeemer. It begins after the Fall and is progressively revealed through the biblical covenants, reaching its fulfillment in Jesus Christ and the New Covenant.

**Key passages:** Genesis 3:15; Genesis 15:6; Habakkuk 2:4; Romans 4:1–8; Galatians 3:6–29; Hebrews 8:6–13.

Under covenant theology, the Noahic, Abrahamic, Mosaic, Davidic, and New Covenants are often understood as successive administrations or historical expressions of the one Covenant of Grace. Traditions differ on how these covenants relate to one another, but Christ is understood as the fulfillment of God's saving promise.

## Pactum salutis — Covenant of Redemption

The *pactum salutis* means “covenant of redemption.” It is a theological term describing the eternal purpose shared by the Father and the Son concerning the salvation of sinners.

The Bible does not explicitly use the phrase *pactum salutis*, but the concept is drawn from passages showing that:

- The Father gives and sends the Son: John 6:38–40; John 10:36.
- The Son willingly accepts His mission: Hebrews 10:5–7; Philippians 2:6–8.
- The Son offers Himself for His people: Isaiah 53:10–12; Hebrews 10:10–14.
- The Father promises the Son glory and a kingdom: Psalm 2:7–8; Philippians 2:9–11.
- The Son completes the work given by the Father: John 17:4; John 19:30.

The *pactum salutis* is not a contract between two separate gods. The Father and the Son are one in divine nature, while the Son willingly takes the role of the obedient Mediator in the incarnation. This eternal purpose is fulfilled historically through the New Covenant.

## Marriage covenant

Marriage is explicitly called a covenant:

- Genesis 2:24 — husband and wife become “one flesh.”
- Proverbs 2:17 — marriage is described as “the covenant of her God.”
- Malachi 2:14 — the wife is called a covenant partner, with God as witness.
- Matthew 19:4–6 — Jesus teaches that God joins husband and wife together.
- Ephesians 5:25–33 — marriage reflects Christ's sacrificial love for the Church.

The marriage covenant involves exclusive faithfulness, lifelong commitment, mutual love, and becoming one flesh. It is a human covenant made before God and is distinct from the *pactum salutis*. Marriage also serves as a picture of Christ's relationship with His people (Isaiah 54:5; Hosea 2:19–20; Revelation 19:6–9; 21:2, 9).

## Other explicit human covenants

The Bible also records covenants or formal agreements between people and nations:

| Covenant | Main passages | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Abraham and Abimelech** | Genesis 21:22–34 | A covenant concerning peace and the possession of a well. |
| **Isaac and Abimelech** | Genesis 26:26–33 | A peace agreement between Isaac and the Philistine ruler. |
| **Jacob and Laban** | Genesis 31:43–55 | A covenant establishing boundaries and peace. |
| **Jonathan and David** | 1 Samuel 18:1–4; 20:16–17; 23:18 | A covenant of loyal friendship and family protection. |
| **Israel and the Gibeonites** | Joshua 9:3–27 | Israel makes a covenant of peace with the Gibeonites. |
| **Joshua and Israel** | Joshua 24:25 | Israel renews its commitment to serve the Lord. |
| **Jehoiada and the people** | 2 Kings 11:17; 2 Chronicles 23:16 | The people covenant to belong to the Lord and reject Baal. |
| **Josiah and Judah** | 2 Kings 23:1–3; 2 Chronicles 34:29–33 | National renewal of obedience to God's Law. |
| **Zedekiah's slave-release covenant** | Jeremiah 34:8–22 | Judah covenants to release Hebrew slaves, then violates the covenant. |
| **Salt covenant** | Numbers 18:19; 2 Chronicles 13:5 | “Covenant of salt” emphasizes permanence and faithfulness. |

## Covenant storyline

**Creation → Fall → Noah → Abraham → Israel and the Law → David and the King → Christ and the New Covenant**

The central movement is:

- **Abrahamic covenant:** God forms a people and promises blessing to the nations.
- **Mosaic covenant:** God gives His covenant people His Law.
- **Davidic covenant:** God promises a righteous, everlasting King.
- **Pactum salutis:** The Father and Son purpose to accomplish redemption.
- **New Covenant:** Jesus fulfills redemption and gives forgiveness and a new heart to His people.

## Key distinction

The **pactum salutis** describes God's eternal purpose of redemption. The **New Covenant** is the historical covenant Jesus establishes with His people through His blood:

> “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.” —Luke 22:20
