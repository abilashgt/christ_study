# Genesis and the Ten Commandments

## The right relationship

The best word is **foundation**—more precisely, **theological foundation**.

Genesis does not formally give the Ten Commandments, nor does it simply “apply” them. Instead, Genesis establishes the truths that the commandments later codify and apply within Israel’s covenant.

> **Genesis lays the theological foundation for the Ten Commandments, while Exodus 20 formalizes and applies those principles within Israel’s covenant.**

The Ten Commandments are given most directly in the context of redemption:

> **Exodus 20:1–2** — “I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.”

Thus, Genesis supplies the creation and moral foundations, while Exodus 20 gives the covenant commandments explicitly.

## Relationship to the creation order

The theological foundations of the Ten Commandments come from the creation order established in Genesis 1–2. They are not founded on later history. The historical narratives of Genesis 3–11 illustrate humanity’s violation of that order, while Exodus 20 formally expresses its moral implications within Israel’s covenant.

### Creation order: Genesis 1–2

- **God as Creator** — God alone is worthy of worship (**Genesis 1:1**).
- **Humanity made in God’s image** — human life has unique dignity (**Genesis 1:26–27**).
- **Marriage as a one-flesh union** — marriage is protected by the prohibition against adultery (**Genesis 2:24**).
- **Work and seventh-day rest** — the Sabbath reflects God’s pattern of creation and rest (**Genesis 2:2–3**).
- **Family relationships** — the family structure provides a basis for honoring parents (**Genesis 2:24; 4:1**).

### Historical illustration: Genesis 3–11

These narratives do not establish the theological foundation; they illustrate how sin violates the created order:

- **Coveting and disobedience** — Eve desires and takes the forbidden fruit (**Genesis 3:6**).
- **Murder** — Cain kills Abel (**Genesis 4:8–10**).
- **Violence and corruption** — the earth becomes filled with violence (**Genesis 6:5, 11–13**).
- **Pride and rebellion** — humanity seeks independence from God at Babel (**Genesis 11:1–9**).

Therefore, the Ten Commandments may be understood as the covenantal formulation of creation principles, given to a sinful people whom God redeemed from Egypt.

## Genesis foundations for the commandments

### 1. No other gods

- **Genesis 1:1** — “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.”
- **Genesis 35:2–4** — Jacob tells his household to put away their “strange gods.”

### 2. Do not make idols

- **Genesis 31:19** — Rachel takes her father’s household idols.
- **Genesis 35:2** — “Put away the strange gods that are among you.”

### 3. Do not misuse God’s name

- **Genesis 4:26** — People “began to call upon the name of the LORD.”
- The command is stated explicitly in **Exodus 20:7**.

### 4. Keep the Sabbath

- **Genesis 2:2–3** — God rests on the seventh day and blesses it.

### 5. Honor father and mother

- **Genesis 9:20–27** — The contrast between honoring and dishonoring Noah illustrates the importance of respecting parents.

### 6. Do not murder

- **Genesis 4:8–10** — Cain murders Abel.
- **Genesis 9:6** — “Whoso sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.”

### 7. Do not commit adultery

- **Genesis 2:24** — “They shall be one flesh.”
- **Genesis 39:7–9** — Joseph refuses adultery, calling it sin against God.

### 8. Do not steal

- **Genesis 30:33** — Jacob speaks of honesty regarding what belongs to Laban.
- **Genesis 31:19** — Rachel steals her father’s household gods.

### 9. Do not bear false witness

- **Genesis 12:10–20** — Abraham’s deception in Egypt causes trouble.
- **Genesis 27:18–24** — Jacob deceives Isaac.

### 10. Do not covet

- **Genesis 3:6** — Eve sees, desires, and takes the forbidden fruit.
- **Genesis 13:10–11** — Lot chooses land based on its attractive appearance.

## Main commandment passages

- **Exodus 20:1–17** — The Ten Commandments.
- **Deuteronomy 5:6–21** — Moses repeats the Ten Commandments.

## Summary

Genesis provides the theological foundation and anticipates the moral principles of the Ten Commandments. Exodus 20 formalizes those principles as Israel’s covenant law.

## Word usage

- **Foundation** — the best word; Genesis establishes the theological basis for the commandments.
- **Anticipates** — Genesis points forward to the commandments.
- **Grounds** — Genesis provides their basis.
- **Prefigures** — Genesis contains patterns that later become explicit.
- **Transcends** — not the best word here; it means “goes beyond,” rather than “provides the foundation for.”
