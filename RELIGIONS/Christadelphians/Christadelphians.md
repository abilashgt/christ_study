# Christadelphians

Christadelphians are a 19th-century restorationist Christian movement that emphasizes the Bible as the only authoritative source of doctrine. They are non-trinitarian, reject an immortal soul, and teach that salvation is tied to faith, repentance, and baptism by immersion.

## Core Beliefs

| Topic | Christadelphian view |
| :---- | :---- |
| God | One God, the Father alone; reject the Trinity |
| Jesus | The promised Messiah and Son of God; fully human, not co-equal with the Father |
| Holy Spirit | God’s power, not a separate person |
| Scripture | The Bible is inspired and sufficient for doctrine |
| Salvation | Faith, repentance, and baptism by immersion are essential |
| Death | The dead are unconscious until resurrection; no immortal soul |
| Future hope | Return of Christ, resurrection, judgment, and God’s kingdom on earth |

## Distinctives

- No ordained clergy or central hierarchy.
- Strong focus on personal Bible reading and doctrinal precision.
- Emphasis on a literal future kingdom of God on earth.
- Rejects eternal torment as the final destiny of the wicked.

## Historical Notes

- Originated in the 1800s around John Thomas.
- The name "Christadelphian" means "brothers in Christ".
- The movement developed formal statements of faith to define fellowship.
