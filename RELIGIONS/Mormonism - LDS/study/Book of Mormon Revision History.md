# Book of Mormon Revision History

The Book of Mormon has had multiple published editions. If counted by major editions, there are **8 editions total**, which means **7 revisions after the original 1830 edition**.

## Major Editions

| Year | Edition | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1830 | First edition | Original publication in Palmyra, New York |
| 1837 | Second edition | Major grammar and wording cleanup; Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery were involved |
| 1840 | Third edition | Further corrections and rewording |
| 1841/1842 | British editions | Based largely on the 1840 text |
| 1879 | Orson Pratt edition | Introduced chapter-and-verse formatting |
| 1920 | LDS standardization edition | Important editorial standardization |
| 1981 | Study edition | Added modern study aids such as headings, footnotes, and cross-references |
| 2013 | Updated edition | Mostly formatting and study-aid updates |

## Summary

- Most changes were **grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting**.
- The core narrative and doctrine were largely preserved.
- If someone asks “how many revisions in total,” the clean answer is **7 revisions** after the first edition.

## Revision Deltas

| From | To | Main delta |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1830 | 1837 | Major proofreading pass: grammar, spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and some wording standardization |
| 1837 | 1840 | Further corrections; Joseph Smith compared the text against the original manuscript, so this edition is often treated as the last one he personally supervised |
| 1840 | 1841/1842 | Mostly reprint-level changes for British publication, with spelling/typography normalization rather than major textual revision |
| 1841/1842 | 1879 | Big presentation change: Orson Pratt reorganized the book into shorter chapters, added verses, and introduced footnotes |
| 1879 | 1920 | Editorial standardization: double-column layout, refreshed punctuation and footnotes, and other publication cleanup |
| 1920 | 1981 | New LDS study edition: introductions, chapter summaries, footnotes, and cross-references; the base text stayed largely the same |
| 1981 | 2013 | Mostly minor corrections and study-help updates; no major doctrinal or narrative rewrite |

## Practical Read

- **Textual revisions** were concentrated in the **1830, 1837, and 1840** editions.
- **Formatting and study aids** changed most in **1879, 1920, 1981, and 2013**.
- If you want the biggest single structural shift, it was **1879**, when the book got the chapter-and-verse system used today.

## 1830 To 1837

The 1837 Kirtland edition was a revision of the 1830 first edition, using the printer's manuscript plus editorial emendations and grammatical corrections.

### Main Types Of Changes

- **Spelling standardization**
- **Capitalization cleanup**
- **Punctuation changes**
- **Grammar fixes** such as verb agreement, pronouns, and sentence structure
- **Occasional wording clarifications** where the earlier wording sounded awkward or ambiguous in modern English

### What Did Not Change

- The overall narrative
- The book order
- The core doctrinal content
- The witness statements and title-page material

### Scope Note

The 1830 to 1837 difference is large at the line level, but most changes are minor. A complete verse-by-verse list is best handled by a textual collation rather than a summary note.
